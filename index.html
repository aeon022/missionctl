Frequently asked

missionctl is a suite of 9 local-first terminal apps — mail, calendar, tasks, notes, budget, diary, time tracking, habits, and social posting — that expose 67 MCP tools so an AI assistant can act on your behalf directly from your own machine. All data stays local; there is no missionctl cloud service, and every app is free to download and use.

MCP is an open protocol, created by Anthropic, that lets AI assistants call external tools — like reading your calendar or sending an email — through a standard interface. Every missionctl app ships its own local MCP server (<app> mcp) so an MCP-capable client such as Claude Desktop can use it directly.

It lets your AI assistant actually do the busywork instead of just discussing it — draft and send email replies, triage your calendar and find free slots, keep your task list current, log time and generate invoices, write a developer diary from your git history — through natural conversation instead of switching between apps.

All 9 apps are free to download and use (MIT licensed). A one-time $39 Bundle (or $9 per feature individually) unlocks optional AI features in 5 of the apps — no subscription either way. For the AI client side: Claude Desktop's free plan already supports local MCP servers, so no paid Claude plan is required. missionctl's own AI features also work with a free-tier Gemini key or a fully local Ollama model at no cost at all.

notectl, budgetctl, diaryctl, timectl, and habctl work on macOS and Linux. mailctl now does too, via a Thunderbird backend (currently in testing — see the mailctl page for details). calctl and taskctl still require macOS (they bridge Calendar and Reminders).

All data stays on your machine. missionctl never calls any external server. The only network calls are what you explicitly trigger (sending an email, publishing a post, or an optional AI call).

mailctl and calctl launch Apple Mail and Calendar in the background via AppleScript if they aren't open. No manual intervention needed.

Yes. Every tool has a full CLI (mailctl inbox --json, taskctl today, etc.). The TUI is the default but optional.
